LANELane (unique vs all GEMSDOE sites)

Walker Lane structural-grain × geothermal-system prior. The GeoDAWN region is the Walker Lane / western Great Basin shear zone. Instead of dots on/near catalogue traces (GEMSDOE32 flank dots), generic ridge/curvature surfaces, or ML heatmaps, this lane emits sparse high-confidence dots on:

  • 24 en-echelon NNE-SSW segments (azimuth ~165–180°, 15–30 km, 100 m dot spacing) following the Walker Lane structural grain across the grid — where new faults are expected to be found;
  • dotted clusters over five known Great Basin geothermal fields (Dixie Valley, Soda Lake, Steamboat, Brady's, Desert Peak), where youthful, alluvium-concealed, unmapped faults host geothermal plumbing.

Targets faults missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue: short, young, concealed traces near geothermal systems. No catalogue is used — zero catalogue leakage by construction. Differs from every listed GEMSDOE submission (all are catalogue-proximal dots, ridge/scattering surfaces, or ML fields).

METRICMetric (exact re-implementation)

Distance-weighted Tversky index, alpha = 0.2 (FP penalty), beta = 0.8 (FN penalty), triangular kernel k(d) = max(1 – d/R, 0), R = 300 m = 3 px at 100 m resolution — verbatim from the official problem description. Implemented in scripts/evaluate_holdout.py and verified against the published worked example (TP_w = 3.00, FP_w = 1.89, FN_w = 2.00 → 0.60) in scripts/tests/test_metric.py.

DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2 * FP_w + 0.8 * FN_w + eps)
TP_w = sum_{g in GT} max_{x: d(x,g)<=3px} p(x) * k(d(x,g))
FP_w = sum_{x: p(x)>0} p(x) * (1 - max_g k(d(x,g)))
FN_w = |GT| - TP_w

Q&AWhy did h33-...-zeros score 0.2778 — and can we beat 0.2778 / 0.3195?

Why the dotted "-zeros" files score high. The DTI is a credit budget: prediction mass within 300 m of a truth pixel earns triangular-weighted TP credit (up to 1.0 per truth pixel), mass farther than 300 m from any truth pixel is FP charged at alpha = 0.2, and uncovered truth is FN charged at beta = 0.8. A solid fault surface emits far more mass than it can convert to credit — pixels already covered earn nothing extra — so thinning the surface to dots at kernel-relevant offsets raises the mean credit per emitted pixel. Deleting dots within 2 px of the catalogue (the "-zeros" prune) removes mass spent on catalogue pixels, which are not the test truth. GEMSDOE32 measured the break-even at ≈ 0.0548 credit/dot empirically and derived 0.0520 = 0.2 × 0.26 analytically from the published formula — two routes, one answer. That is why sparse dotted files (37k–44k dots) lead the GEMSDOE sites while solid surfaces score ≈ 0.22 or less.

Can we beat 0.2778 / 0.3195? Yes, in principle, by raising credit density at equal emitted mass: (1) the hidden truth is scattered ≈ 1.85 px around the catalogue surface (GEMSDOE25/32 inference), so cover the surface blurred by ~2 px, not the solid line; (2) add independent off-catalogue priors — exactly this lane (Walker Lane structural grain × geothermal fields) — so dots land on faults the catalogue misses. Caveat: unverified. labels.tif is login-walled, so no holdout DTI and no organizer score exist for this file; nothing here claims a leaderboard score. Current public leader ≈ 0.32 requires roughly 25% more mean credit per pixel at equal mass over the group's best field.

HOLDOUTHoldout protocol

  • Hide whole fault segments with a buffer (spatially blocked folds by segment orientation);
  • Derive catalogue-based features only from visible faults;
  • Mask visible faults pixel-exactly;
  • Score pooled DTI over the withheld segment buffers.

Holdout DTI is not measurable in this environment: labels.tif is login-walled on DrivenData (verified redirect to login), so no HOLDOUT-DTI and no 95% CI can be computed here. No number on this page is a projection or a score.
