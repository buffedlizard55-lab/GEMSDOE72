H1 — Dilatational step-over / relay-ramp fracture zones (top candidate)

Mechanism. Geothermal permeability is focused where fault segments step over or relay; these zones host distributed dilatational fractures rather than a single mapped trace, so they are systematically absent from a discrete-trace catalogue.

Layers. Geodetic dilatation rate, shear strain rate, second invariant of the strain-rate tensor; total-magnetic-intensity vertical and horizontal gradient (magnetic edges mark the bounding faults).

Why off-catalogue. USGS/INGENIOUS map traces; a step-over fracture zone is a permeability fabric with no through-going line — the catalogue omission is the signal.

Differs from repo. The repo baseline emits dots with no physical detector; this targets strain-localization geometry computed from the geodetic + magnetic bands.

Named non-fault mimic. Sedimentary basin-edge flexure / alluvial-fan coalescence can produce broad low-strain zones that look like step-overs without a fault.

Validation. Spatially-blocked holdout: withhold whole fault segments with a buffer; derive catalogue-based features only from visible faults; mask visible faults pixel-exactly; score pooled DTI. Leakage canary: any single feature with holdout AUC > 0.90 is leakage until proven otherwise.

Data needed. Competition training features (login-walled). No new external data required for H1.

Priority: validate first · cost: medium

H2 — Hydrothermal demagnetization halos

Mechanism. Hydrothermal alteration destroys magnetite, producing a low-magnetic-amplitude halo around faults and geothermal systems that is broader than the fault trace itself.

Layers. Reduced-to-pole anomaly, total magnetic intensity, TMI vertical/horizontal gradient, depth to magnetic source.

Why off-catalogue. The halo extends beyond the mapped trace; catching the alteration envelope targets off-catalogue area.

Named non-fault mimic. Lithologic magnetic lows (e.g., felsic intrusions, sedimentary cover) produce demag-like signatures without a fault.

Priority: second · cost: low-med

H3 — Conductivity-surface anomalies

Mechanism. Clay alteration and hydrothermal fluids raise bulk electrical conductivity; the conductivity surface band is a direct geothermal indicator that need not coincide with a mapped fault.

Layers. Conductivity surface; depth to conductive (basement) surface.

Named non-fault mimic. Saline groundwater / conductive sedimentary basins produce broad conductivity highs unrelated to faulting.

Priority: third · cost: low

H4 — Seismicity lineaments

Mechanism. Microseismicity swarms delineate active structures too young or small for Quaternary catalogue mapping.

Layers. Distance to earthquake, earthquake density; supplemented by USGS ComCat.

Named non-fault mimic. Volcanic or induced seismicity clusters not on a fault plane.

Data needed. USGS ComCat (reachable from official sources but timed out from this sandbox).

Priority: fourth · cost: med-high · needs ComCat

H5 — Detrended-elevation scarps + curvature

Mechanism. Recent faulting leaves subtle scarps/lineaments in detrended elevation and its slope, blurred at 100 m resolution.

Layers. Detrended elevation; detrended elevation slope; 1 m DEM links for finer curvature.

Named non-fault mimic. Erosional / depositional geomorphology (alluvial scarps, landslide scarps).

Data needed. 1 m DEM links from the competition (login-walled) + DEM processing.

Priority: fifth · cost: high · needs 1 m DEM
