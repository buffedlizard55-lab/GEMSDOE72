Why geothermal vents live on faults (and why the catalogue is incomplete)

Geothermal systems in the Great Basin are fault-hosted: Basin-and-Range extension creates normal faults that act as permeable conduits for meteoric water → heats at depth → returns along fault damage zones. Key synthesis: Faulds et al. (2015) Geothermal play fairway analysis, Great Basin — structural permeability at step-overs, relay ramps, and terminations controls fluid flow; Faulds & Hinz (2015) GES — 60% of known fields sit at fault intersections/step-overs.

The USGS Quaternary Fault & Fold Database (QFFD, usgs.gov) maps faults with Pleistocene surface rupture evidence. It is systematically incomplete under Quaternary alluvium (Qal) — young cover buries scarps within decades (slip rates 0.5–2 mm/yr). The INGENIOUS project (gbcge.org) and GeoDAWN airborne surveys (geodawn, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ) were flown to see through cover via magnetics/radiometrics — but mag sees basement, not shallow structure.

Implication: Hidden faults are disproportionately geothermal (they are the ones under cover where fluids pond). The test set — “faults that do not appear in the public USGS database but were manually identified by fault experts” (problem description) — is enriched in geothermal-coincident structures.

What others overlook — our contrarian but grounded angle

Prior high scorers (H19-5 0.1922, H25-1 0.2477, D28 0.2600, H32-D 0.2632, H33-2-B2 0.2778) all share one lane: ridge/scarp lineaments on topography or magnetics (Frangi/Sato Hessian at 1–3 px). They optimize packing (dotted vs solid) and budget (44k vs 88k). None target alteration halos.

Geothermal literature says alteration halos are the direct fluid proxy:

  • Smectite-illite clay caps produce conductive MT anomalies (cond_surf low) + K-depletion (radiometric K low, Th/K high) — Stanley et al. MT; Hase et al.
  • Fault damage zones have 1–3 km broad topographic flexure (hanging-wall syncline / footwall arch) even when 100 m scarp is absent — Oskin et al. Walker Lane; Wesnousky & Theberge (2020) QFFD bias under cover.
  • Radiometric Th/K ratio discriminates clay alteration from lithology: Thorium is immobile, K is leached → Th/K highs mark alteration — Beamish (2013) airborne radiometrics; GeoDAWN radiometric extensions (Th/K, U/K, U/Th) are provided as geodawn_extensions_u8.tif (derived in GEMSDOE24, sha256 a35a9cd...).

Contrarian hypothesis: The highest leverage is not tighter packing of the same ridge field, but a different physics that is geothermal-specific and cover-penetrating — radiometric + MT + broad topography — where catalogue faults are blind.

Why 0.2778 → 0.3262 happened (and how to beat it)

See README §Why. In brief: 0.2778 was thinning + strict off-catalogue buffer (mechanism: DTI budget). Leaders added scatter-aware packing (1.85 px smoothing, +0.0247 MC, 12/12 draws). We add scattered physics (alteration halos are scattered ~200 m around the trace, same kernel scale) — so smoothing the fused field is still optimal, but the field itself is geothermal-specific.

Estimated ceiling: At 0.3262, mean k ≈0.065; break-even bar = α·s = 0.065. Our field's top 42k mean credit is ~0.058 at current mass — need ~12% higher credit density. Alteration halo edge is narrower (higher k when on-trace) but rarer — so budget-matched emission must be selective (our greedy 2 px spacing does this).

Hypothesis specifications — what was built and measured

See hypotheses.html for 5 hypotheses. H72-A built as src/gems72/h72.py: Roberts cross (Th/K edge) + Sobel (cond) + Laplacian divergence (σ=10 px) + zero-crossing (σ=2 px), robust-unit normalized (median/IQR), fused via geometric mean, ≥2 agree (dilated 4/6 px), greedy max-coverage 2 px spacing, 2 px catalogue buffer.

Cost to implement H72-A: Low — uses 19 official bands + geodawn extensions already mirrored (no new download). H72-C/D need external fetch (3DEP/GDR) — scripts ship, not run (budget).

Holdout — does H72 beat the incumbent's own rule?
